---
title: "Chapter 4 : When i went Home"
---
Angela walked home with slow, mechanical steps, each movement heavy as if her body carried the weight of the world. Her dress clung to her skin, stained with dirt and something darker, a silent testament to what had just happened. Her eyes were fixed on the ground; she couldn’t bear to look up. She didn’t want to see the familiar world of the villa, the marble floors, the sparkling chandeliers—it all felt alien now, irrelevant to the storm inside her. She felt hollow, like a shadow of herself. If she moved slowly enough, maybe the world would forget she existed. The house waited, polished and untouchable, untouched by the filth clinging to her soul. A perfect mansion with golden lights and parents who measured love in reputation, not affection. The maid glanced at her as she passed, her eyes flicking over the torn sleeve, the bruised knees, before sliding away, indifferent. No one stopped her; no one asked. 

Angela crossed the hallway in silence, a ghost among the living. “If they didn’t see me… maybe it wasn’t real,” she thought, clinging to the illusion that invisibility could erase what had happened. She locked herself in the bathroom, the cold tile under her bare feet grounding her in a small fragment of reality. The mirror stared back, old and merciless, reflecting a girl she barely recognized. Skin scrubbed raw, hands shaking under scalding water, claws scraping at her flesh as if she could erase the fingerprints no soap would wash away. Tears finally came, silent and relentless, tracing down her cheeks without a sound. Just like her, just like the life she no longer felt she had. Upstairs, her parents laughed with guests over expensive wine, their voices distant and indifferent. Downstairs, her world collapsed in a bathroom no one would ever notice, no one would ever understand. 

That night, a part of Angela died. The part that survived learned to fake it—to smile, to stay silent, to hide. She didn’t tell a soul, not even her sister Amelia. Under silk sheets, while the household slept, she wept quietly, her sobs muffled by the pillow, her small body shaking with grief and fear. A fragile, broken girl remained—a shadow of her former self, carrying a secret pain that would define the years to come. Pain and silence became her companions, and loneliness her closest friend.
